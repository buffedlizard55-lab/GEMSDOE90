1.1 Exact Metric Operator: Pooled Distance-Weighted Tversky Index ($\alpha = 0.2, \beta = 0.8, R = 300\text{ m}$)

Let $\Omega \subset \{0,\dots,3729\}\times\{0,\dots,3291\}$ denote the active `5,167,373`-pixel GeoDAWN survey footprint (`100 m` resolution in `EPSG:32611`), $C \subset \Omega$ denote the `60,988`-pixel mapped USGS/INGENIOUS fault catalogue (`existing_faults.tif`), $A = \Omega \setminus C$ denote the `5,106,385` active evaluation pixels (known faults masked pixel-exactly with zero buffer), $G \subset A$ denote newly discovered ground-truth faults, and $P: A \to [0, 1]$ denote predicted fault confidence. With linear triangular radial kernel $k(d) = \max(0, 1 - d / 3\text{ px})$ over Euclidean pixel distance $d \le 3\text{ px}$ (`25` integer offsets inside $5 \times 5$):

s_G(y) = max_{x in G} k(||y - x||_2),    s_P(x) = max_{y in A} [ P(y) * k(||x - y||_2) ]
TP_w = sum_{x in G} s_P(x),    FN_w = |G| - TP_w,    FP_w = sum_{y in A} P(y) * (1 - s_G(y))
DTI(P, G) = TP_w / ( TP_w + 0.2 * FP_w + 0.8 * FN_w ) = TP_w / ( 0.8 * |G| + 0.2 * TP_w + 0.2 * FP_w )

Three structural properties of this operator govern every empirical jump on the leaderboard:

  1. Non-Additive Max-Plus Coverage on $TP_w$ vs. Additive Linear Penalty on $FP_w$: Placing two adjacent unit pixels $P(y_1)=P(y_2)=1$ along a ridge increases $FP_w$ linearly (`+2.0` on unrewarded ground), whereas $s_P(x) = \max_y P(y)k(\|x-y\|)$ takes only the pointwise maximum over the 25-pixel disk! Continuous ribbons (`120k–220k` pixels) pay `3x–5x` redundant $FP_w$ for overlapping 300 m disks.
  2. Binary $\{0, 1\}$ Optimality Theorem (Subfractional $p \in (0, 1)$ Dominance): For any pixel $y$, scaling $P(y) = p$ scales its marginal contribution to both $TP_w$ and $FP_w$ linearly in $p$ whenever $p \cdot k$ wins the local `max`, and contributes strictly zero to $TP_w$ while still paying full $0.2\,p$ to $FP_w$ whenever a nearby `1.0` dot dominates it! Thus soft probabilities ($p \in (0.05, 0.8)$) strictly lose to sparse, non-redundant binary $\{0, 1\}$ allocations.
  3. Asymmetric Known-Catalogue Flank Penalty ($0 < d(y, C) \le 2\text{ px}$): As confirmed by organizer chrisk-dd (Thread #11516), $C$ is masked pixel-exactly (`0 px` buffer). Predictions at $1 \le d(y, C) \le 2\text{ px}$ (`100–200 m` parallel to an already-mapped fault) are not masked out and incur full $FP_w = 1.0$ unless a genuinely new splay or unmapped strand lies within 300 m. Because potential-field and topographic ridges naturally halo mapped faults, unpruned flanks hemorrhage $FP_w$.

4. Geothermal Vents, Structural Permeability & Hydrothermal Demagnetization in the Great Basin

Approximately 75% of known Great Basin geothermal systems are blind (lacking active hot springs or fumaroles at the surface; INGENIOUS / Faulds et al., 2021). In northwest Nevada's extensional-transtensional transition between the Walker Lane dextral shear belt and the Basin and Range province, convective hydrothermal upflow is localized not along planar mid-segment fault cores (which often seal via clay gouge and silica cementation), but inside specific structural dilatational traps where fracture aperture stays critically stressed:

  1. Horsetail Fault Terminations & Anderson Mode-II Wing-Crack Cones (Curewitz & Karson, 1997; Faulds & Hinz, 2015): Near the propagating tip of a slipping fault of length $L$, elastic crack-tip tensile stress scales as $\sigma_{\theta\theta}(r, \theta) \propto K_{II}/\sqrt{2\pi r} \cdot (-3\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\cos^2\frac{\theta}{2})$, peaking along oblique dilatational splay fans ($\theta_0 \approx \pm 25^\circ\text{–}35^\circ$) rather than dead-ahead at $0^\circ$. High fracture density in these horsetail damage zones hosts major Great Basin geothermal upflow plumes (e.g., Desert Peak, Bradys, San Emidio).
  2. Releasing En-Echelon Step-Overs & Relay Ramps: Where two sub-parallel normal or oblique-slip faults overlap across a `0.4–2.8 km` step-over, bed rotation and breaching fractures maintain sub-vertical permeability pipes connecting `2–4 km` deep basement reservoirs to shallow aquifers (`vis_relay_bridge` in `H90-1`).
  3. Hydrothermal Demagnetization Troughs & Potassic / Clay-Cap Alteration (`TMI_up150 - RTP`, `K/Th`, `cond_surf`): Ascending $150\text{–}260^\circ\text{C}$ neutral-chloride and steam-heated geothermal fluids oxidize primary ferrimagnetic titanomagnetite ($\text{Fe}_{3-x}\text{Ti}_x\text{O}_4$) in Neogene basalts and ash-flow tuffs into paramagnetic pyrite ($\text{FeS}_2$), hematite ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$), and smectite-illite-adularia clays. Consequently, active and fossil hydrothermal fault conduits manifest in GeoDAWN's high-resolution drape magnetics as narrow shallow magnetic lows/troughs that attenuate rapidly in `150 m` upward-continued `TMI_up150`, accompanied by airborne radiometric potassium (`K`) / `K/Th` enrichment along exhumed adularia-sericite footwalls and elevated shallow magnetotelluric conductivity (`cond_surf`, Band 17) over smectite clay caps.
  4. Quaternary Volcanic Vents & Silica/Cation Geothermometry (`GDR 1391`): Within the GeoDAWN footprint, `21` mapped Quaternary-Neogene volcanic vents and `27,092` thermal/groundwater sampling wells (`temp_c`, quartz `geothermquartz_c`, chalcedony `geothermchalc_c`, and Na-K-Ca `geothermcat_c` geothermometers) trace deep crustal magmatic/amagmatic heat flux feeding concealed basin-margin and intrabasin fault conduits.

6.1 Explicit Scientific & Operational Limitations (`IRREGULARITIES.md`)

  • Holdout-to-Leaderboard Domain Shift (`IR90-38`, `IR90-47`): `gems57-pooled-hide-v2` withholds already-mapped USGS/INGENIOUS segments (biased toward range-front scarps), whereas live DrivenData evaluation scores newly compiled unmapped faults (richer in concealed intrabasin and subtle LiDAR/aeromagnetic structures). Therefore `HOLDOUT-DTI` (`0.10681`) is strictly a paired model-selection statistic, never a numerical forecast of live leaderboard DTI.
  • Incomplete 1 m 3DEP LiDAR Footprint (`75.4%` coverage): `24.6%` of the GeoDAWN footprint lacks 1 m LiDAR tiles; in those blocks `H90-1` conditions explicitly on `lidar_valid = 0` and shifts weight to `100 m` `det_elev`, `rtp`/`TMI_up150` magnetics, `iso_grav_anom`, and radiometrics.
  • Non-Fault Geological Confounders: Pleistocene Lake Lahontan beach berms (`~1,330 m` shoreline elevation) and remanently reverse-magnetized Tertiary ash-flow tuff cooling margins can mimic scarp/magnetic-trough signatures; `second_best_domain` corroboration suppresses single-sensor false positives but cannot eliminate multi-sensor volcanic caldera ring margins without seismic reflection data.
  • CPU Sandbox vs. Multi-Scale 2D Vision Backbones: Training occurred on a 2-vCPU / 3.8 GiB CPU sandbox (`388 s`); closing the remaining gap toward `0.4413` (`H90-5`) will likely benefit from multi-fold GPU 2D ConvNeXt/U-Net++ segmentation trained end-to-end on `DW-Tversky(0.2, 0.8)` patches combined with our CELF `R = 300 m` submodular dot allocator.
