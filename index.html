Why this file exists (the short version)

The official metric is a distance-weighted Tversky index, DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw) with a 300 m triangular kernel. Because FNw = |G| - TPw and α+β=1, it collapses to

DTI = T / (0.2·T + 0.2·(S - M) + 0.8·|G|)

where T is coverage credit on the hidden faults, S is total predicted mass and M is the part of that mass sitting close to truth. 0.8·|G| is a constant, so a dot helps only if the credit it adds beats 0.2θ/(1-0.2θ) — about 0.059 credit at θ=0.2778. Mass is bang-bang (always emit 1.0 or nothing) and the dot budget is therefore a quantity the metric decides, not a style choice.

Inverting that identity against the owner's own scored rasters shows the incumbent family has been buying credit at a price barely above break-even: see the table below.

Calibration: can any model of the hidden field reproduce real scores? (NEGATIVE)

Two independent attempts, both constrained to a physically valid occupancy field (0 ≤ π ≤ 1, Σπ = |G| — a pixel either is a hidden-fault pixel or it is not):

  1. Learner field. π ∝ pmodelγ, γ and |G| fitted. Best: γ = 6.00, |G| = 31,362, RMSE 0.0609.
  2. Model-free band inversion. π = ρband·[(1−η) + η·w/w̄], with ρ solved by non-negative least squares per distance-to-catalogue band and η measuring how much the learner's ranking helps. Best: η = 1.00, |G| = 34,657, RMSE 0.0853, leave-one-out RMSE 0.1109.

Both fail, in the same direction: every targeted raster is under-predicted by roughly a factor of two (the incumbent most of all). Dropping the occupancy cap produces an apparently excellent fit (RMSE 0.015) only by letting single pixels carry a density of 70 hidden faults — an artefact, not a result. The honest conclusion is that where those dots sit is not explained by distance-to-catalogue or by the current 63-feature stack, so no leaderboard projection is made.

Every scored raster in the registry is a halo around the existing catalogue. This candidate is not: it places 96% of its mass in ground the catalogue does not touch, which is where a genuinely undiscovered fault has to be. That is also why it is risky — the collar strategy is the one with real score feedback behind it, and this one has only the holdout.
